A manager asks the Brunel graph a question in plain English. A language model writes the SPARQL, the graph's own description keeps it honest, and when the graph cannot answer, it says so. Then: running it for real, and what the field has not solved.

What is new

A layer that turns a question in English into a query, shows its evidence, refuses what it cannot answer, and is measured like any other model.

Objective

By the end you can build a question answering layer over a graph, make it check and repair its own queries, make it refuse, and measure it on a test set.

Part 1 of 5 · about 15 minutes

A language model writes SQL and SPARQL fluently. Why not just ask it?

Because it writes about the graph it imagines, not the one you have.

The gap this session closes

The manager's sentence and the query that answers it share almost no words

“How many orders did carrier V44_3 carry?”

SELECT (COUNT(?order) AS ?n) WHERE {
  ?order ul:carriedBy
    <https://ul.edu.lb/kr/id/carrier/brunel/V44_3> .
}
Defined on this slide
Text to SPARQL
Turning a question in plain language into a SPARQL query, here by a large language model.
What the writer must know

The property is ul:carriedBy, not “carry”. The carrier is an IRI built from its code. Neither is in the question.

Three failures, each real on the Brunel graph

Only the first one produces an error you would notice

It invents a property

ul:shippedBy sounds right. The graph says ul:carriedBy. The query runs and answers nothing.

It misses a subclass

Asked how many orders customer V555_15 placed, it wrote ?order a ul:Order: 0. All 110 are typed ul:LateOrder only.

It answers the unanswerable

“Which customers are in Beirut?” The graph has no places at all, but a query can still be written.

Orders found by ?o a ul:Order9,023All orders9,215
Defined on this slide
Hallucination
A model stating something fluent that is not true: here, a property or an answer the graph does not have.

Part 2 of 5 · about 20 minutes

What does the model need to know about the graph, and how little is enough?

Its classes and properties, and a few worked examples. Not the whole ontology.

What make_void.py writes

For each class: how many things. For each property: from which class, to which.

<.../brunel/void>
    void:classPartition ul:Plant-partition .
ul:Plant-partition void:class ul:Plant ;
    void:entities 19 ;
    void:propertyPartition ul:Plant-makes .
ul:Plant-makes void:property ul:makes ;
    void:triples 2035 ;
    void:classPartition [ void:class ul:Product ] .
Defined on this slide
VoID
Vocabulary of Interlinked Datasets: a small RDF description of a graph's classes, properties and counts.
Small on purpose

189 triples describe all 135,841. One SPARQL CONSTRUCT writes them, in 0.3 seconds, in the layout of SIB's void-generator.

schema_text(): VoID turned into plain lines

One line per class, one line per property: 41 lines for the whole graph

ul:Order (9,023 things)
ul:Product (1,540 things)
ul:RateBand (1,540 things)
ul:LateOrder (192 things)
ul:Customer (46 things)
...
ul:Order ul:carriedBy ul:Carrier
ul:Order ul:lateDays xsd:integer
ul:Plant ul:makes ul:Product
ul:Plant ul:servesPort ul:Port
Why plain lines

A model reads text. “ul:Order ul:carriedBy ul:Carrier” says which way the link goes and what is at the other end: all a query writer needs.

The counts help too

ul:LateOrder (192 things) sitting next to ul:Order (9,023) is a hint that orders come in two kinds.

examples.ttl · six worked examples

A question and its query, stored as RDF, so tools can find and share them

  rdfs:comment
    "Which products does plant PLANT08 make?" ;
  sh:select """
SELECT ?product WHERE {
  <.../plant/brunel/PLANT08> ul:makes ?product .
}""" .
Defined on this slide
Few-shot examples
Worked question and query pairs placed in the prompt for the model to imitate.
Why the SHACL vocabulary

sh:SPARQLExecutable is how SIB's endpoints publish their examples, and what sparql-llm loads. Your examples become reusable data.

For “How many orders did carrier V44_3 carry?”: the words it shares with each example

The three with most shared words go into the prompt: 1, 2 and 4

ExampleIts questionShared words
1Which orders did carrier V44_3 carry, with their service level?4: carrier, carry, orders, v44_3
2How many orders and how many late orders does each carrier have?2: carrier, orders
4How many orders are there in total, late ones included?1: orders
6How many orders did customer V555_15 place with service level DTP?1: orders (a tie, later in the file)
3, 5Which products does plant PLANT08 make? · Which ports does ...0
A simple rule, on purpose

Common words (how, many, did) are ignored first. sparql-llm does the same job with embeddings of the questions; word overlap needs no download and always picks the same three.

The whole prompt for one question

Nothing hidden: python show_prompt.py prints every character

Prompt

Three test questions the graph cannot answer

Defined on this slide
Refusal
Saying the graph cannot answer, and why, instead of guessing.
Two ways to refuse

The rules say: reply REFUSE: <what is missing>. And if no valid query survives two repairs, the layer refuses for the model.

Grounding and citation

Never a bare number: the query and the rows come with it

question: How many orders did carrier V44_3 carry?

  ?o ul:carriedBy <.../carrier/brunel/V44_3> }
answer: 1 row

Anyone can check it

The query is the citation: an analyst can read it, run it on the endpoint, and see 854. A wrong query is visible, not hidden in a sentence.

Defined on this slide
TEXT2SPARQL
A yearly benchmark for text to SPARQL systems, with a fixed contract and scorer.
Why a contract

The harness runs the returned query itself and scores the rows. So the system is judged on answers, not on how it got them.

Defined on this slide
Execution accuracy
Judging a query by the answer it returns, not by its text.
Why not compare query text?

The same answer has many correct queries. Only the answer can be compared fairly.

Defined on this slide
Data drift
The data changing over time until what was true of it no longer is.
One query can cross departments. The layer must not.

Access control on a graph, and what the model is allowed to see

Ask as the user

The layer queries with the asker's rights, never an administrator's. Fuseki can limit each user to named datasets and graphs.

Send the schema, not the data

The model gets VoID and examples. The rows stay in your stack. A free tier's inputs may be used to improve the provider's products.

Keep keys out of code

The key lives in .env, which is never committed. A key in a script or a slide is a leaked key.

Defined on this slide
Access control
Rules on who may read or change which data.
Six questions the field has not settled · about 22 minutes

This course checked every answer after the fact. What would it take not to have to?

Six open questions. Each team picks one for its report.

Open problem 1 of 6

Ontology learning from text

Can a model write the ontology Session 3 wrote by hand, from documents?

Why it is open

Models now suggest classes and relations well enough to help, but not well enough to trust: someone must still decide what the words mean.

Read

Asim and others, Database, 2018 (a survey) · Babaei Giglou, D'Souza and Auer, “LLMs4OL”, ISWC 2023

Open problem 2 of 6

Constraints by construction

Can a learned model obey a rule because of how it is built, not because we check its output?

Why it is open

Sessions 4 and 7 check afterwards: SHACL, the query check. Building the rule into training is possible for simple logic, hard for real shapes.

Read

Xu and others, “A semantic loss function”, ICML 2018 · Giunchiglia and Lukasiewicz, JAIR, 2021

Open problem 3 of 6

Do models reason over ontologies?

When a model scores well on a benchmark, did it reason, or remember?

Why it is open

A model may have seen the benchmark in training. Today's score, like ours, shows answers, not how they were reached.

Read

He and others, “Language model analysis for ontology subsumption inference”, Findings of ACL 2023 · Sainz and others, Findings of EMNLP 2023

Open problem 4 of 6

Time and space

How do you ask when something was true, or how near two things are?

Why it is open

This course left them out: Brunel has one order date. Facts that change over time, and places, need more than plain triples.

Read

Gutierrez, Hurtado and Vaisman, IEEE TKDE, 2007 · OGC GeoSPARQL 1.1, 2024

Open problem 5 of 6

Reproducible results

Do reported gains in knowledge graph completion survive a fair comparison?

Why it is open

Several did not: tuned old models matched new ones. Session 6's baseline was this lesson on our own data.

Read

Kadlec, Bajgar and Kleindienst, 2017 · Ruffinelli, Broscheit and Gemulla, ICLR 2020 · Sun and others, ACL 2020

Open problem 6 of 6

Standards in motion

What do you build on when the standard is still changing?

Why it is open

SHACL 1.2 Core is a W3C Working Draft (18 September 2026). The RML Working Group is not yet chartered; its charter was being refined from June 2026.

Read

W3C, SHACL 1.2 Core, Working Draft · W3C strategy issue 443, the RML Working Group charter

Copy the twin, change one thing

def unknown_properties(query, classes, props):
    known = {p.split("#")[1] for p in props}
    used = {t for t in ul_terms(query)
            if t[0].islower()}
    listed = ", ".join(sorted("ul:" + k
                              for k in known))
    return [f"ul:{t} is not a property of "
            f"this graph. The properties are: {listed}"
            for t in sorted(used - known)]
